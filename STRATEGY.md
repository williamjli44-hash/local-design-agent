# Revenue operating plan

## Objective and assumptions

Maximize collected cash profit from useful work, then improve profit per owner hour. The first milestone is one paid, accepted delivery; the second is three repeatable profitable jobs. Running the model longer is not a success metric.

This is a strategy hypothesis, not a revenue forecast. No customers or market demand have been validated by this repository. The owner is technically literate, has a local computer, can review web code, and can spend roughly 30 minutes a day on customer communication plus delivery review. If those assumptions fail, reassess the offer before accepting work.

## First offer: one mobile website repair for $149

Start with small web agencies or site owners **already requesting a specific paid fix**. Examples: a mobile menu overlaps content, a call-to-action disappears at small widths, or a landing page overflows horizontally. Prefer a stack the owner can actually test: HTML/CSS/JavaScript or an existing React project.

The initial $149 price is a test, not a claimed market rate. Sell a concrete repair, not "AI consulting," guaranteed conversions, or a generic report. The deliverable is a patch or pull request, before/after screenshots, test notes, and rollback instructions. Include one revision within the original scope. Start a two-business-day delivery clock only after access, the agreed scope, and payment/milestone arrangements are confirmed. The owner must confirm availability before offering that deadline.

Limit scope to one reproducible issue on one page, initially estimated at no more than two hours of total delivery work. Exclude full redesigns, backend payment work, authentication, SEO ranking promises, accessibility certification, and unfamiliar proprietary site builders. If discovery reveals a bigger problem, re-scope before working further. Never sell a result the current tools cannot deliver.

Why this experiment: a small code patch has a concrete acceptance test, low inventory cost, and can produce evidence for a portfolio. Whether buyers will pay is the question we are testing. Competing offers include DIY and other developers; being powered by a local LLM is not itself a customer benefit.

## Acquisition: respond to demand first

1. Collect up to five recent, relevant **posted paid requests, inbound inquiries, or explicitly invited agency requests** each day. Use the configured web-search tool for initial discovery or owner intake in the dashboard. Search snippets are candidates, not confirmed current jobs. The owner opens the original request before any response is sent.
2. Preserve the source URL, observation date, buyer's actual problem, budget if stated, stack, and any communication restriction. A public website flaw alone does not prove buying intent. Unknown facts stay unknown.
3. Rate each candidate on five factors, each 0–2: explicit willingness to pay; match to our skills; reproducible scoped issue; credible access/payment path; plausible positive margin. A 2 needs evidence, a 1 means uncertain, and a 0 means absent or disqualifying. Draft at most three responses a day for candidates scoring at least 8/10. No candidate qualifies solely because the model likes the idea.
4. Tailor a short response: the buyer's problem, the exact deliverable, one relevant **real** example, price, and one scope question. If we have no customer examples, use a clearly labeled self-created portfolio sample. Do not fabricate experience or testimonials.
5. The owner sends selected responses through the permitted channel, negotiates, and records the result. A saved draft is not a sent message. One follow-up after three business days is a starting hypothesis only if the recipient/platform permits it; stop after rejection or opt-out.

Example response structure (fill facts from evidence):

> Your request describes [specific mobile issue]. I can offer a fixed-scope repair on [page], with a patch, before/after screenshots, and test notes for $149. One revision for that issue is included. [Link to a genuine relevant portfolio sample, if one exists.] Can you confirm the stack and provide a staging copy or repository access? I will confirm the scope and delivery date before we start.

Do not buy mailing lists, generate bulk unsolicited outreach, or run paid ads in the first validation cycle. These add cost before we know whether the offer sells. Marketplace proposal fees, if used, come from the experiment budget and must be measured as acquisition costs. Keep payments and communication within the relevant platform's requirements.

## $200 allocation and economics

Treat $200 as working capital, not revenue. Reserve $150 until the first offer is validated; allow at most $50 of unrecovered validation expense. Proposed experiments are limited to $20 per UTC day, including other pending/approved proposals. Prefer $0 tests. The current tools can reserve a planning budget but do not spend anything. Book actual expenses even if they exceed a plan: hiding a loss makes the next decision worse.

Count ALL paid acquisition, marketplace/payment fees, refunds, hosting, incremental electricity, paid model calls, and rework. Track owner minutes separately. The default owner-time value is $25/hour, an explicit planning assumption that should be changed to match the owner's circumstances. Taxes are not calculated here.

Illustration only, not a prediction or current provider pricing:

| One completed job | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Customer cash collected | $149.00 |
| Hypothetical combined fees at 15% | −$22.35 |
| Direct operating/acquisition expense | −$5.00 |
| Cash contribution before owner time and tax | $121.65 |
| Two owner hours valued at $25/hour | −$50.00 |
| Contribution after imputed owner time, before tax | $71.65 |

Replace the 15% example with the actual quoted fees before pricing. If acquisition takes four more owner hours, contribution after time falls to −$28.35. The true constraint may be customer acquisition, not coding.

For a candidate, estimate: probability of a paid accepted job × contribution if won − prospecting cost. Early probability estimates are guesses; use a pessimistic case and prefer cheap tests. After actual results exist, use observed win rates with the sample size displayed. Do not present three wins as a statistically reliable forecast.

## Work order

Each daily run should prioritize, in order:

1. A paid client's blocked delivery, acceptance, or refund issue.
2. An existing buyer awaiting a scoped answer.
3. A qualified opportunity with recent demand evidence.
4. Improving the portfolio sample or current offer based on a recorded objection.
5. Research only when there is a specific unanswered question that prevents a decision.

Read the account, pipeline, previous drafts, and run history first. Produce at most one experiment proposal and three useful drafts. Reuse the same opportunity/draft kind to revise, not duplicate. If there is no new evidence and no unresolved task, record what is missing and stop. Never keep inventing experiments to look busy.

The daily brief must contain: collected revenue, costs/refunds, cash contribution, owner time, work completed with artifact IDs, top next action, evidence behind that action, and the exact owner decision needed. Distinguish observed facts, estimates, and unknowns.

## First 14 days and decision gates

| Period | Deliverable | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1 | One self-created before/after portfolio sample and written scope | Can the owner reproduce and verify the repair? |
| Days 2–4 | Up to 10 qualified opportunities and tailored draft responses | Are buyers asking for this exact outcome? |
| Days 5–7 | First paid pilot if a buyer accepts; otherwise categorize objections | Fix access/trust/scope before discounting |
| Days 8–14 | Deliver, collect, and ask satisfied buyers about another scoped fix | Repeat only when the actual job has positive contribution |

These are work targets, not promises of sales. With zero substantive replies after 10 well-matched owner-sent responses, change one variable: buyer segment, example, or opening. With replies but no sale, classify price/trust/timing/scope objections. After 20 qualified owner-sent responses or 14 days of actual outreach with no paid pilot, stop spending and review the offer with the owner. Do not silently reset the count by renaming the offer.

After three accepted paid jobs with positive contribution after owner time, test $199 on the next five comparable quotes. Keep scope constant. Seek repeat work from the same agency; sell a maintenance package only when requests recur and actual delivery economics justify its price. A template/product can follow repeated demand; do not build a speculative SaaS product before this evidence exists.

## Delivery checklist

Confirm acceptance criteria and access. Reproduce the problem on a staging copy. Make the smallest patch. Check mobile and desktop widths, keyboard interaction, relevant tests/build, and absence of unrelated regressions. Have the owner review the patch and evidence. Customer acceptance and payment are separate events; neither can be inferred from a model's answer. Preserve backups and handoff instructions. Do not publish customer material in a portfolio without permission.

The current agent can plan and draft; it has no browser or code-execution tools for customer work. Until these are added and verified, implementation and deployment are owner tasks or are performed through a separately authorized coding assistant.

## Increase autonomy when it improves measured economics

This workflow uses source-linked demand evidence and recorded receipts. Increase autonomy when it reduces measured cost or delay without lowering delivery quality. A $200 account balance alone does not cap liabilities, refunds, fees, or contractual commitments. The model can research and prepare work; sending offers, agreeing contracts, and deploying customer work depend on the owner's accounts and authority.

- [Upwork Project Catalog](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/360057397533-How-to-create-a-project-in-Project-Catalog) documents packaging a service with defined price, scope, delivery days, and revisions. It does not establish demand or earnings for this offer.
- [Ollama tool calling](https://docs.ollama.com/capabilities/tool-calling) documents the model/tool-result loop. The runner also checks the installed model's reported tool capability.
- [REVIEW.md](REVIEW.md) records what was missing in the original project and what remains outside this implementation.

This plan is intentionally specific enough to falsify. Keep the financial facts immutable, record losses and failed tests, and change the strategy when the buyer evidence contradicts it.
